# Flagged uncertainties from the research phase

Points the research agents flagged as ambiguous or uncertain. Resolve or hedge before publishing.

- The WebFetch summaries paraphrase some passages; I quote-marked only wording the fetches returned as quotations (e.g. Nielsen's 'word salad'/'Jabberwocky', IBM's 'coin on its edge', Aaronson's 'fundamental misstep' and the 'random answer' line). For a public explainer, the exact wording of these specific quotations should be re-verified against the live pages before quoting, as the fetch model may have lightly normalised phrasing.
- Quantum Country is a long, paywalled-spaced-repetition essay; the fetch returned mainly Part I/II for the main essay. The claim that it 'defers entanglement until after single-qubit fluency' and treats interference geometrically is well-supported by the search essay fetch, but the precise placement of entanglement within qcvc was inferred from truncated content rather than read in full.
- Pérez-Salinas et al. (1907.02085) was published in Quantum 4, 226 (2020); I cite the arXiv version. The exact journal volume/page were not re-fetched from the published record, only from secondary recall plus the arXiv abstract, so confirm the Quantum citation details before formal referencing.
- The IBM module author attribution (Katie McCormick) came from a video-walkthrough credit reported by the fetch, not from a byline I read directly; treat the authorship as tentative.
- Matt Strassler and Chris Ferrie are individual expert bloggers (well-regarded but not peer-reviewed); their framings are useful and broadly consistent with the consensus but should be presented as commentary rather than canonical sources.
- There is genuine, live debate in the literature/interpretation community about whether interference 'requires' a Many-Worlds reading (Aaronson is deliberately agnostic; Deutsch is not). Our explainer should stay at the amplitude/interference mechanism level and flag interpretation as contested rather than asserting a stance.
- The two time-series regression benchmarks (arXiv:2504.12416 and the QCHALLenge benchmark arXiv:2412.13878) appear to be preprints that I did not confirm as peer-reviewed; treat their specific numbers as indicative rather than settled. I quoted their conclusions from search summaries and did not WebFetch the full PDFs.
- Bowles, Ahmed & Schuld (2403.07059) is a benchmark of CLASSIFICATION tasks, not regression. Its headline findings (classical > quantum; entanglement often unhelpful) are widely taken to transfer to regression, but that transfer is an inference, not something the paper itself proves for regression.
- The dequantization/surrogate results (Sweke et al. 2025; Schreiber et al. 2023; Cerezo et al. 2025) apply most cleanly to encodings giving POLYNOMIALLY many Fourier frequencies (e.g. Pauli/single-qubit re-uploading). Whether models with exponentially rich, controllable spectra can be both trainable AND advantageous is genuinely open; the literature does not rule out advantage there, it shows the easy/common cases are reproducible classically.
- Whether VQCs offer a GENERALISATION (sample-complexity) advantage rather than an accuracy/expressivity advantage is contested and unresolved; several authors (including Schreiber et al.) flag it as the remaining open avenue, but I found no clean demonstration of such an advantage on real-world regression data.
- I confirmed exact wording for the foundational and theoretical papers via WebFetch of arXiv/journal abstract pages, but did not read full PDFs end-to-end, so fine-grained technical conditions (exact frequency-count thresholds, precise design recommendations) should be checked against the full texts before being quoted verbatim in the public explainer.
- Page/article numbers for some venues (e.g. the Cerezo 2021 Nature Communications article number) were taken from search-result metadata; worth a final cross-check against the publisher page before publication.
